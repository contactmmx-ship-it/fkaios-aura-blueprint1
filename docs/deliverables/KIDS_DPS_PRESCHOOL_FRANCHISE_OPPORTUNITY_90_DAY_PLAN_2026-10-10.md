# Kids DPS — Preschool Franchise Opportunity & 90-Day Action Plan
**Research snapshot:** 10 October 2026  
**Purpose:** Establish a fact-disciplined decision brief for evaluating whether Kids DPS can become a repeatable preschool franchise in India.  
**Evidence rule:** Competitor information below is drawn from linked public pages. Where the page does not display a publication date, it is labelled **publication date unavailable**; 10 October 2026 is the access date, not the publication date. Brand network sizes, awards, investment ranges and marketing claims are attributed to the brand and are not independently audited. No Kids DPS fee, outlet economics, market share or performance is assumed.

## Executive decision

Kids DPS should be treated as a **franchise system to be proven**, not yet as a franchise offer that is ready for sale. The available public evidence shows a competitive category with several established franchise networks, curriculum-led positioning, and varying site/investment requirements. It does not establish Kids DPS's own unit economics, parent demand, safety performance, curriculum outcomes or repeatability.

### Five important findings

1. **The category has established franchise operators.** Kidzee publicly describes 2,500+ centres in 600+ cities and an established pedagogy/support model; these are the brand's own claims and need independent diligence before being used as audited market facts. [Kidzee franchise page](https://www.kidzee.com/partner-with-kidzee) (publication date unavailable; accessed 10 Oct 2026).
2. **Publicly advertised investment and space requirements vary materially.** EuroKids advertises ₹15–20 lakh and a minimum 1,500 sq ft; Little Millennium lists ₹15–30 lakh depending on location/size and a minimum property size of 2,000 sq ft. These are indicative brand-published claims, not comparable all-in project budgets. [EuroKids](https://www.eurokidsindia.com/franchise-business-opportunities/) and [Little Millennium](https://www.littlemillennium.com/franchise-with-us/) (publication dates unavailable; accessed 10 Oct 2026).
3. **The category is not one uniform franchise model.** Hello Kids advertises a no-royalty model, while KLAY describes itself as a non-franchised chain. Kids DPS must define its intended model—franchise, managed centre, owned centre, or a staged hybrid—before pricing or partner recruitment. [Hello Kids](https://www.hellokids.co.in/franchise.php); [KLAY](https://klay.co.in/join-us/) (publication dates unavailable; accessed 10 Oct 2026).
4. **Curriculum is a stated differentiator, but proof is required.** Competitors publicly cite Playway/Montessori, Seven-Petal, PéNTEMiND and thematic/holistic approaches. Kids DPS needs a documented curriculum map aligned to foundational-stage goals, observed child-development evidence, teacher training and consistent delivery—not only a syllabus or marketing promise. [NEP 2020](https://www.education.gov.in/sites/upload_files/mhrd/files/nep/NEP_Final_English.pdf).
5. **Safety and compliance must be engineered into the operating system.** National policy and child-protection guidance provide important reference points, but a specific centre's legal obligations depend on its state, local authority, premises, services and operating model. A location-specific compliance matrix and documented safety controls must precede any public franchise promise. [NCPCR regulatory guidelines for private play schools](https://www.ncpcr.gov.in/uploads/165753774662cc04d28580b_regulatory-guidelines-for-private-play-schools-by-national-commission-for-protection-of-child-rights-85-kb.pdf); [NCPCR school safety manual](https://ncpcr.gov.in/uploads/165650439662bc404c9314e_manual-on-safety-and-security-of-children-in-schools-sep-2021.pdf).

## 1. Market evidence: facts vs hypotheses

### Evidence observed

- The official brand pages linked in the competitor table advertise franchise or operating models, curriculum approaches, partner support, site requirements or network claims.
- Publicly advertised investment figures are not directly comparable unless they define the same scope: brand fee, fit-out, lease deposit, furniture, learning materials, technology, pre-opening payroll, marketing, taxes and working capital.
- NEP 2020 sets a policy direction for a stronger foundational stage and early-childhood care and education. It is a policy document; it does not by itself prove demand, willingness to pay or the profitability of any particular Kids DPS location.
- NCPCR has published regulatory guidelines for private play schools and a school safety manual. The relevant local legal status and applicability must be checked for the proposed centre and services.

### Hypotheses to test—not established market facts

- Parents in a target catchment may value visible safety controls, communication, teacher consistency and measurable learning more than a broad marketing claim.
- A curriculum that makes age-appropriate learning visible to parents may improve trust and referrals.
- A standardised operating model may make multi-centre quality easier to reproduce, but only if the pilot demonstrates fidelity and sustainable economics.
- Local demand, fee acceptance, competitor density and seasonality cannot be inferred from national brand presence. They require a catchment study, parent interviews and paid-intent testing in the actual target city/locality.

**Market-data limitation:** This brief does not present a national preschool market-size estimate, growth rate, Kids DPS market share, local enrolment forecast or revenue forecast because a sufficiently reliable, directly attributable dataset has not been established here.

### What this comparison means for Kids DPS

Do not compete on “more centres” or a low headline investment unless Kids DPS can substantiate the claim and deliver the same scope. A defensible position to test is:

1. **Safety made visible:** documented arrival/release, visitor control, incident response, staff screening, emergency drills, premises checks and parent communication.
2. **Curriculum made observable:** weekly learning intentions, age-appropriate activity plans, child observation records and periodic parent conferences.
3. **Franchise operations made repeatable:** centre opening checklist, staffing and training standards, lesson-delivery observations, audit cadence, parent complaint closure and unit-level economics.
4. **Transparent economics:** a written investment schedule, working-capital assumptions and fee/royalty definitions, with no earnings guarantee.
5. **Evidence-led claims:** publish only what the pilot has measured and what source records can substantiate.

These are proposed differentiation hypotheses. They are not yet proven Kids DPS advantages.

## 3. Regulatory and operational workstream

This section is an operating checklist, not legal advice or a declaration that every item is universally mandatory in every Indian state.

### National reference points

- **National Education Policy 2020:** use the foundational-stage and early-childhood-care-and-education policy direction to shape the learning framework and age-appropriate pedagogy. Source: [Ministry of Education — NEP 2020 PDF](https://www.education.gov.in/sites/upload_files/mhrd/files/nep/NEP_Final_English.pdf).
- **NCPCR private play-school guidelines:** use as a key child-protection and play-school regulatory reference. Verify the applicable status, state adoption/implementation and local rules for the target location. Source: [NCPCR regulatory guidelines PDF](https://www.ncpcr.gov.in/uploads/165753774662cc04d28580b_regulatory-guidelines-for-private-play-schools-by-national-commission-for-protection-of-child-rights-85-kb.pdf).
- **School safety:** use the NCPCR safety manual as a control-design reference; confirm which provisions apply to a preschool/daycare format and which are guidance. Source: [NCPCR safety manual PDF](https://ncpcr.gov.in/uploads/165650439662bc404c9314e_manual-on-safety-and-security-of-children-in-schools-sep-2021.pdf).
- **Government communication:** the PIB release on play-school guidelines is useful context, but it must not substitute for checking the current law, state circulars and municipal rules. Source: [PIB release](https://www.pib.gov.in/Pressreleaseshare.aspx?PRID=1780966&lang=2&reg=48).

### Location-specific due diligence before a centre opens

Assign a qualified local adviser to determine, in writing, the applicability and process for:
- play-school/ECCE registration or recognition, if applicable;
- municipal land use, trade/establishment permissions and building occupancy;
- fire and emergency requirements for the actual premises;
- lease, society/landlord permissions and permitted use;
- staff qualification, background verification, child-protection and reporting procedures;
- sanitation, drinking water, food service and daycare requirements if food or extended care is offered;
- transport, CCTV, child data/privacy, consent, incident logs and secure child handover;
- employment, payroll, insurance, tax, consumer disclosures and franchise-contract requirements.

For every item, the compliance register should record: jurisdiction, exact rule/circular, issuing authority, URL/document, effective date, applicability decision, responsible owner, evidence required, renewal date and local legal sign-off. Do not label a guideline “universally mandatory” without the evidence to support that conclusion.

|---|---|

## 5. 90-day action plan — three 30-day phases

All numerical thresholds below are **proposed management targets**, not industry benchmarks or guaranteed results. Confirm the actual starting baseline in Days 1–10 and adjust targets transparently.

### Days 1–30 — Define and de-risk

**Milestones**
- Confirm the target city/locality and pilot centre format.
- Complete the Kids DPS data room: ownership/brand rights, curriculum, fees, admissions, costs, staff, premises, parent feedback, incident history and current policies.
- Map the curriculum to age groups and foundational-stage outcomes; identify what is designed, delivered and assessed.
- Complete the location-specific compliance register with local adviser review.
- Conduct a competitor/catchment scan using named centres, fees where publicly available, parent interviews and visit observations.

**Proposed KPIs**
- 100% of required internal data fields assigned an owner and status (received / missing / not applicable).
- 100% of identified compliance items have an applicability decision or named adviser/deadline.
- At least 15 structured parent/prospect interviews and 5 competitor observations, if the local research sample is feasible.
- No external financial or regulatory claim published without a source and owner approval.

**Dependencies:** access to internal records; brand-owner decisions; target location; local adviser; permission to interview parents/visit centres.

### Days 31–60 — Engineer and prove the pilot system

**Milestones**
- Draft the centre operations manual and daily/weekly checklists.
- Implement child arrival/release, visitor, incident, emergency, hygiene and parent-communication protocols.
- Create lesson-plan templates, child-observation records and teacher coaching rubrics.
- Define the unit-economics model using actual local quotes and Kids DPS data; show downside/base/upside scenarios, with assumptions visible.
- Train staff and run observed simulations before relying on the system.

**Proposed KPIs**
- 100% of pilot staff trained and assessed on their assigned safety-critical procedures before unsupervised duty.
- 100% of scheduled safety checks recorded during the test period.
- At least 90% completion of sampled lesson-plan/observation records during the trial; improve any gap before expansion.
- All high-severity safety or compliance gaps closed before the pilot is represented as franchise-ready.

**Dependencies:** pilot staff, curriculum owner, facilities, approved SOPs, actual cost quotations and documented escalation ownership.

### Days 61–90 — Measure, rectify and decide on replication

**Milestones**
- Run the pilot/operational rehearsal for a representative period; capture exceptions and parent feedback.
- Conduct an independent quality and safety audit; log corrective actions, owners and closure evidence.
- Reconcile actual cost and operational data against the unit-economics assumptions.
- Update manuals, training, launch checklists and franchise support responsibilities.
- Hold a formal gate review: proceed to a second pilot, extend the proof period, or stop and redesign.

**Proposed KPIs**
- At least 90% parent satisfaction on a clearly defined survey question with response count reported; treat this as a target, not a forecast.
- 100% of incidents logged and triaged; zero unresolved critical safety findings at the replication gate.
- At least 90% adherence on the sampled operating audit, with critical controls treated as pass/fail.
- Every open corrective action has an owner, due date and evidence requirement.
- Unit economics reconciled to actuals; no franchise sales/ROI promise until the evidence gate passes.

**Dependencies:** sufficient operating exposure to test the model; reliable records; independent reviewer; owner decision on go/no-go.

## 6. Information Kids DPS must supply before a financial commitment or franchise launch

### Brand and curriculum
- Legal owner of Kids DPS name, marks and curriculum; registration/ownership evidence and permission to franchise.
- Current curriculum by age band, lesson plans, learning outcomes, teacher guides, materials and evidence of delivery.
- Actual teacher recruitment, qualification, training, assessment and ongoing coaching standards.
- Existing centre list and status; no centre count should be published without a reconciled register.

### Current operating evidence
- Centre-level enrolment by month and age group; enquiries, conversions, retention, withdrawals and attendance.
- Fee schedule, discounts, refunds, receivables and parent complaints.
- Staffing roster, payroll, attrition, substitutes, training hours and supervision span.
- Premises lease, area, fit-out, maintenance, safety inspection, incident and emergency-drill records.
- Parent feedback with sample size, survey dates and response rate.

### Commercial model
- Itemised start-up cost with supplier quotations; lease deposit, fit-out, furniture, learning resources, technology, pre-opening payroll, marketing, taxes and working capital shown separately.
- Monthly fixed/variable costs; actual fee collection and centre-level cash flow.
- Franchise fee, royalty, marketing fund, renewal/transfer terms, territory, required purchases and support obligations.
- Sensitivity analysis for low admissions, delayed opening, staff-cost increases and rent escalation.
- Written policy on what the franchisor does versus what the franchisee must execute.

### Legal and safeguarding
- Target states/cities and intended services (preschool only, daycare, meals, transport, extended hours).
- Current legal/compliance register and local permissions.
- Child safeguarding policy, staff background-check process, authorised pickup, incident escalation, data access/retention and emergency contacts.
- Insurance, complaint handling, escalation and audit arrangements.

**Decision rule:** if critical evidence is missing, label it “not supplied” and make it a gate dependency. Do not replace it with an industry average or an invented Kids DPS figure.

## 7. Go/no-go gate at Day 90

**Proceed to controlled replication only if:**
1. Brand/IP authority and intended franchise model are documented.
2. The curriculum and teacher-delivery system are documented and demonstrated in the pilot.
3. Location-specific compliance and child-safety controls have evidence and no unresolved critical gaps.
4. The pilot's operational and parent-feedback data are complete enough to evaluate.
5. Actual cost assumptions and downside scenarios have been reviewed; the business model is not dependent on unsupported enrolment or ROI claims.
6. A franchise support owner, audit cadence, corrective-action process and partner agreement are ready.

Otherwise, extend the pilot or redesign. A missed gate is not a failure of ambition; it is a signal that the system is not yet proven for replication.
